Why · the case for deployment

Every company can now buy intelligence. If everyone can access it, intelligence alone cannot be the moat. So the advantage moves into deployment.

The same foundational capability is becoming available to anyone able to pay for it. The edge is no longer who has intelligence — it is where, how, and why they use it. Someone has to decide where intelligence belongs. That person is the forward deployed engineer.

What this page is

The method I use to take an AI system from someone's messy real workflow into their production estate — and the deliverable that closes each stage.

It is deliberately unglamorous. Most AI projects do not fail on model quality; they fail because nobody audited the real workflow, nobody agreed what "working" meant, or nobody could deploy it inside the customer's security boundary.

Each stage earns the next

No building before the audit is signed off. No deployment before the evals pass. No hand-over before their team has run it themselves. Gates, not phases.

Built to hand over

The engagement ends with the customer's own people operating the system — documented, monitored and owned by someone who is not me.

The job · three parts

Each stage earns the right to the next.

Part 01

Audit

Identifies the right problem and maps reality. The documented process is rarely the real process, so the audit determines what should be automated before anyone begins building.

  • Interviews, emails, spreadsheets
  • SOPs, systems, approvals
  • Exception paths
  • Bottlenecks & repetitive work
  • Judgment points & failure modes
Part 02

Evals

Turns non-determinism into evidence. A graded set of cases — normal, edge, incomplete, ambiguous, high-risk — produces a pass rate, failure categories and thresholds you can argue from.

  • Right data · required steps
  • Matches expert · safe to act
  • Pass rate & failure taxonomy
  • Confidence thresholds
  • Escalation rules
Part 03

Deployment

Makes it work inside the business. Build over current data and systems, prove it in a sandbox, then grant authority only as reliability is demonstrated.

  • Existing data & internal APIs
  • Identity & permissions
  • Sandbox → production users
  • Human review, logs, alerts
  • Rollback

The audit artifact · one operating map

  1. Current-state workflow — how the work actually happens today.
  2. Future-state workflow — the same work, rebuilt around AI.
  3. Selected use case — one workflow, chosen for value.
  4. Boundaries — what the system may and may not do.
  5. Expected business value — hours, cost and errors, quantified.

The decision · what should be automated

Option 01

Deterministic software

When the rules and inputs are predictable.

Option 02

An agent

When the objective is clear but the inputs, path, or required actions vary.

Option 03

A human in control

When the decision carries material ambiguity, accountability, or irreversible consequences.

Prioritise lengthy, high-volume workflows where the improvement is large enough to matter.

Deployment · make it work inside the business

Where software begins carrying operational responsibility.

  1. Integrate with what already existsBuild over the current data and systems rather than beginning with a large replacement project.
  2. Test inside a controlled environmentUse a sandbox within the company's infrastructure to run, inspect and debug safely.
  3. Increase autonomy graduallyBegin with the smallest useful action and grant more authority only after the system proves reliable.

The timeline · a twelve-week engagement

Four gates. Nothing starts until the last one closes.

Engagements vary, but the shape does not. Each block ends with something the customer can hold — a signed statement, a working prototype on their own data, an evaluation report, a system their team is running without me.

Gate 01 · Week 2Problem signed off
Gate 02 · Week 4Prototype on real data
Gate 03 · Week 8Evaluated system
Gate 04 · Week 12In production, handed over

Find the workflow worth rebuilding.

By week 2 we agree what we are building and what it is worth — in writing, before a line of production code exists.

Into production, then out of my hands.

By week 12 it is live inside their estate and their team is running it. If that has not happened, the engagement has not finished.
